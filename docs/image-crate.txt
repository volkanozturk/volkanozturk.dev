IMAGE CRATE — volkanozturk.dev article artwork
==============================================

docs/design-standards.md is authoritative. It is the single source of truth for
how this site looks and how its images are made.

This file is a portable brief for article-image creation: a self-contained
summary to hand to whoever draws the artwork, or to paste into a tool that has
no access to the repository. It restates the standards; it does not extend them.
Where the two disagree, docs/design-standards.md wins — check it before
starting, and change it there first if a rule genuinely moves.


WHAT EACH ARTICLE NEEDS
-----------------------

Two images per published article:

  1. a square thumbnail
  2. a horizontal cover

They are two separate compositions of the same concept, each designed for its
own aspect ratio around the same recognisable subject. Keep their subject,
palette, and visual language consistent so readers recognise the same article.
Do not create one format by automatically cropping or stretching the other.


THUMBNAIL
---------

  Export      768x768 WebP
  Frontmatter thumbnail: /images/covers/<name>.webp
  Used on     Home and Writing listings
  Displayed   80px tile on desktop, 64px tile on mobile
              (a 1px border leaves the image itself 78px / 62px)
  Loading     lazy

Compose for the small tile. One clear subject, generous weight, no fine detail
— at 64px it is the only size most readers ever see it at.


COVER
-----

  Export      1280x720 WebP (16:9)
  Frontmatter cover: /images/covers/<name>-cover.webp
  Used on     the article page, once, between the header and the body
  Displayed   maximum 640px wide on desktop; on mobile it uses the available
              article-content width — there is no hard 320px cap
              (at a 390px viewport with 16px gutters that is 358px)
  Loading     priority / preload (the article cover keeps this behaviour)

The cover keeps its own aspect ratio, stays centred, and sits with 30px of
space above and below, a 1px border and 10px corners. The wider format has room
for a relationship — a before and an after, a route between two places, a flow
from one thing to another — that the square tile cannot hold.


FALLBACK AND PLACEMENT
----------------------

- When `cover` is absent the article falls back to `thumbnail`, shown at its
  square proportions and compact size. Keep that fallback working; do not
  remove it.
- With neither field the article renders no image container at all.
- Never repeat the cover inside the Markdown body. Setting the two frontmatter
  values is the whole job.
- In-body photographs and technical diagrams are independent of both fields and
  are unaffected by any of this.


ARTWORK DIRECTION
-----------------

The image should communicate the article's actual topic through recognisable
objects and the relationships between them — a browser window for a piece about
layout, a queue draining beside one that does not for consumer lag, two places
and a route for moving country.

  Style      modern minimal editorial illustration
  Ground     warm off-white
  Forms      charcoal and muted grey
  Accent     restrained burnt orange
  Texture    subtle paper-like texture is fine

Avoid: unrelated abstract decoration that could belong to any article, stock or
photorealistic imagery, glossy 3D, neon, heavy gradients, logos and branded
screenshots. No readable titles inside the artwork — the page already has a
heading. Any text at all must be short and mean something to that article.


FILES AND EXPORT
----------------

- Keep the full-resolution original of every approved image. Exports are
  derived from it; the original is the master.
- Export the optimized WebP only after the artwork is visually approved.
- Strip EXIF/ICC/XMP metadata. Judge the result by how it looks, not against an
  arbitrary byte target.
- Keep the dimensions declared in code consistent with the actual files. If an
  export size changes, update the width/height attributes in the same change.
- Images are served as drawn: colours are preserved in dark mode, never
  inverted, recoloured or filtered. Do not design one that depends on being
  flipped.
- New visuals require approval before they replace an existing approved asset.
